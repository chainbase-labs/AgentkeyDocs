Target's assortment is curated rather than exhaustive, and a large share of
it is Target's own brands — home, apparel and grocery lines that exist
nowhere else — which makes target.com a reference for style-led shopping
research as much as for price checks. There is no public product API, and
the storefront blocks automated browsing. Through AgentKey your agent reads
public Target listings the same way it reads everything else — `find_tools`,
`describe_tool`, `execute_tool` — search, product pages and customer reviews.

## What you can ask

- *"Search Target for a 4-cup coffee maker under 40 dollars, best sellers first."*
- *"Find throw pillows on Target, highest rated first, and list the brands."*
- *"What's new in kids' sneakers on Target? Title, price and brand."*
- *"Open this Target product page — price, brand, rating, description and images."*
- *"Is this Target listing one of Target's own brands or a national brand?"*
- *"Pull the most helpful one-star reviews for this Target product."*
- *"What do the newest reviews of this Target crib say about assembly?"*
- *"Compare the price of AirPods Pro at Target, Walmart and Best Buy right now."*
- *"Target 上这款咖啡机最近的评价怎么样？"*

## What's covered

**Search**
- Keyword search with page controls and a price range
- Sort by best match, best sellers, price, rating or newest
- Title, price, rating and brand for each result

**Product details**
- One product by its page URL — title, price, brand, rating, description and
  images

**Reviews**
- Customer reviews for a product by URL, a page of ten at a time — rating,
  title, text, date, reviewer and verified-purchase badge
- Newest first (the default), most helpful, or highest or lowest rating

**Store and currency**
- Coverage is target.com, Target's US site, priced in US dollars

To check a Target price against other retailers, pair this page with
[Walmart](/capabilities/ecommerce/walmart-product-data-for-ai-agents) and
[Best Buy](/capabilities/ecommerce/best-buy-product-data-for-ai-agents), whose
tools work the same way; for furniture,
[IKEA](/capabilities/ecommerce/ikea-product-data-for-ai-agents) search is the
natural third stop.

## How to use it

Pass the request in plain language and let the router pick the tool:

```text
find_tools(q="newest reviews of this Target coffee maker")
find_tools(q="Target 上这款咖啡机的评价")
find_tools(prefix="ecommerce/target")       # browse everything for Target
describe_tool("<returned Provider/Operation>")
execute_tool("<Provider/Operation>", { ...params })
```

Product and review tools take the product page URL, and search results carry
it, so search → product → reviews needs no manual parsing. Prices come back as
Target shows them, in US dollars, not converted. Always `describe_tool` before
executing: it shows the accepted sort values, the page limits, and the
per-call cost.

<Note>
  **Building with code?** Target is available through the catalog tools above

</Note>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need a Target account or Target Circle membership?">
    No. AgentKey holds the upstream access; your agent only needs your AgentKey
    subscription.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it place an order or check my nearest store's shelf?">
    No. Every tool is read-only — search, product details and reviews — and
    what comes back is what target.com shows online, not the stock of a
    particular store near you.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it tell Target's own brands from national brands?">
    Search results and product details carry the brand Target shows on the
    listing, whether that is a national brand or one of Target's own labels.
    From the brand field alone your agent can filter a result set to own-brand
    items, or set an own-brand price next to the national brand's.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
