Google Play is where Android apps are found, and its listings show something
the App Store does not: an install count. Apps are identified by package name
rather than a numeric ID, and reviews can be narrowed by star rating, keyword,
language, app version and device type before your agent reads a single one.
Through AgentKey your agent reads Google Play search results, listings and
reviews the same way it reads everything else — `find_tools`,
`describe_tool`, `execute_tool` — choosing the store country where it matters.

## What you can ask

- *"Pull the latest one-star Google Play reviews for this app that mention 'crash' or 'login'."*
- *"How many installs does this Android app have, and who is the developer?"*
- *"Search Google Play in Germany for password managers and list their ratings, installs and prices."*
- *"Get this app's Google Play listing — developer contact details, ratings count, description and screenshots."*
- *"Show me Google Play reviews of this app from tablet users, newest first."*
- *"What are Spanish-language reviewers saying about this app's latest version?"*
- *"Pull Google Play reviews of this app from the last 30 days, sorted by rating."*
- *"这款 App 在 Google Play 上最近的差评都在说什么？"*

## What's covered

**Search**
- Apps by keyword in any store country, with a limit on how many results come
  back
- Package name, title, developer, rating, installs, price and category for
  each result

**App listings**
- One app by package name in any store country — title, developer and
  developer contact details, rating, ratings count, installs, price,
  description, screenshots and more
- Price and currency follow the store country you pick

**Reviews**
- Reviews by package name or store URL — rating, review text, date and
  helpfulness votes
- Filtered by star rating, keywords, languages, app version, device type
  (mobile, tablet or Chromebook), an end date, or the last N days
- Sorted by relevance, newest or rating
- A `limit` on how many reviews come back; set it to fetch only as many as
  you need

## How to use it

Pass the request in plain language and let the router pick the tool:

```text
find_tools(q="one-star Google Play reviews for this app that mention crashes")
find_tools(q="这款 App 在 Google Play 上最近的差评")
find_tools(prefix="ecommerce/google-play")  # browse everything for Google Play
describe_tool("<returned Provider/Operation>")
execute_tool("<Provider/Operation>", { ...params })
```

Google Play sits in the E-commerce branch of the catalog. Search results carry
each app's package name, which the listing and review tools take, so search →
listing → reviews needs no manual parsing; the review tool also accepts the
store URL. Search and listings take a store country. Set a `limit` to fetch
only as many reviews as you need. Always `describe_tool` before executing: it
shows the country codes, the review filters and sort values, and the per-call
cost.

<Note>
  **Building with code?** Google Play is available through the catalog tools
  above only; there are no fixed-contract Scenario Methods for it yet. See the
  [Scenario Methods overview](/api-reference/scenario/overview) for the
  platforms that have them.
</Note>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need a Google Play Console account?">
    No. AgentKey holds the upstream access; your agent only needs your AgentKey
    subscription.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it reply to, flag or post reviews?">
    No. Every tool is read-only — search, listings and reviews. Nothing is
    posted, answered or reported.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I find an app's package name?">
    It is the `id` in the app's Google Play URL
    (`play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.example.app`), and the search
    tool returns it for every result; the review tool also accepts the store
    URL as is. The same app on iOS is covered by the
    [App Store](/capabilities/ecommerce/apple-app-store-data-for-ai-agents)
    tools, which take Apple's numeric app ID instead.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
