ASOS is an online fashion retailer that sells through separate country
stores — the UK, the US, Australia, Germany and the rest — each priced in its
own currency, and fashion research turns on something most retail tools leave
out: whether a given size is still in stock. asos.com has no public product
API and blocks automated browsing. Through AgentKey your agent reads public
ASOS listings the same way it reads everything else — `find_tools`,
`describe_tool`, `execute_tool` — choosing the country store on every search
and reading per-size stock from the product page.

## What you can ask

- *"Search ASOS Germany for white sneakers, cheapest first, and show which sizes are in stock for the top result."*
- *"Find Nike hoodies on ASOS UK under 50 pounds, newest first."*
- *"Search ASOS Australia for linen trousers between 40 and 80 dollars."*
- *"Open this ASOS product page — price, brand, colour, and which sizes are left."*
- *"Is a medium of this ASOS jacket in stock, and what colour is the listing?"*
- *"Is this dress cheaper on ASOS US or ASOS UK right now?"*
- *"List the brands behind the first page of ASOS results for 'leather belt'."*
- *"ASOS 英国站这条裙子哪些尺码还有货？"*

## What's covered

**Search**
- Keyword search in one country store at a time, with page controls and a
  price range in that store's currency
- Sort by best match, newest or price
- Title, price, brand and colour for each result

**Product details**
- One product by its page URL — title, price, brand, colour, description,
  images and per-size stock
- The store, and so the currency, is read from the URL, so a US-store link
  returns US pricing and stock

**Stores and currencies**
- The UK store is the default; search takes the store as a country parameter
  and covers the UK, the US, Australia, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the
  Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark and Poland
- Each store is priced in its own currency, and prices come back as that
  store shows them

Not covered today: customer reviews. For what a piece resells for
second-hand, [eBay](/capabilities/ecommerce/ebay-product-data-for-ai-agents)
sold listings are the closest comp; for the wider pattern, see
[product research workflows](/capabilities/ecommerce/product-research-workflows-for-ai-agents).

## How to use it

Pass the request in plain language and let the router pick the tool:

```text
find_tools(q="white sneakers on ASOS Germany, cheapest first")
find_tools(q="ASOS 英国站这条裙子的尺码库存")
find_tools(prefix="ecommerce/asos")         # browse everything for ASOS
describe_tool("<returned Provider/Operation>")
execute_tool("<Provider/Operation>", { ...params })
```

Search takes the store as a country parameter and defaults to the UK; product
details read the store from the URL, and search results carry that URL, so
search → product → size check needs no manual parsing. Prices come back in
the store's currency, not converted. Always `describe_tool` before executing:
it shows the accepted country codes and sort values, the page limits, and the
per-call cost.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need an ASOS account?">
    No. AgentKey holds the upstream access; your agent only needs your AgentKey
    subscription.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can it add to bag, place an order or reserve a size?">
    No. Every tool is read-only — search and product details — and per-size
    stock is what the store shows online at request time. Sizes sell out, so
    re-check before acting on it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which country store does a search use?">
    The UK store unless you pass a country, which selects the US, Australia,
    Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Sweden, Denmark or Poland
    instead. Product details take no store parameter: the store comes from the
    URL, so a link copied from the US store returns US pricing and stock.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
