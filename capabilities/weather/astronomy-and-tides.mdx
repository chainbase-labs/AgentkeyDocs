Photographers want golden hour, sailors want the tide, solar installers want
irradiance, and everyone occasionally wants to know when the moon rises. These
are calculable facts about a place and a time, and QWeather serves them for
any location. Through AgentKey your agent reads them with the same three calls
— `find_tools`, `describe_tool`, `execute_tool`.

## What you can ask

- *"When is sunset in Lisbon on 2 November?"*
- *"What's the moon phase tonight in Sydney?"*
- *"Sun elevation and azimuth at this coordinate at 15:30 local time on 21 June."*
- *"High and low tides at Xiamen for the next three days."*
- *"厦门明天的潮汐时间是几点？"*
- *"Hourly solar radiation forecast for a panel tilted 30 degrees facing south at this site, next 48 hours."*
- *"Golden-hour times for a shoot in Kyoto next Friday."*

## What's covered

**Sun**
- Sunrise and sunset for any location on any date in the next 60 days
- Solar elevation and azimuth for any location at a given date, time and
  timezone, with the observer's altitude taken into account

**Moon**
- Moonrise and moonset and the hour-by-hour moon phase for cities, for any
  date in the next 60 days
- Phases are already correct for the southern hemisphere

**Tides**
- Tide tables for the next ten days worldwide: high and low tide times and
  heights, plus hourly tide levels, for a tide station on a given date

**Solar radiation**
- A radiation forecast for any coordinate at 1 km resolution, up to 60 hours
  ahead, at 15-, 30- or 60-minute intervals
- Optional panel tilt and azimuth so the forecast matches a real
  installation, and an option to express times in local time

## How to use it

Pass the request in plain language and let the router pick the tool:

```text
find_tools(q="sunset time in Lisbon on 2 November")
find_tools(q="厦门明天的潮汐时间")
find_tools(prefix="weather/qweather")      # browse all weather tools
describe_tool("<returned Provider/Operation>")
execute_tool("<Provider/Operation>", { location: "<location id or lon,lat>", date: "2026-11-02" })
```

Sun and moon tools take a location id or coordinates plus a date; the tide
tool takes a tide-station id, found with the POI search described in
[location lookup](/capabilities/weather/location-lookup); the radiation
forecast takes a latitude and longitude. Always `describe_tool` before
executing — the solar-elevation tool needs date, time, timezone and altitude
together.

<Note>
  **Building with code?** Astronomy, tides and solar radiation are available
  through the catalog tools above; there is no fixed-contract
  [Scenario Method](/api-reference/scenario/overview) for them yet. Sunrise,
  sunset, moonrise, moonset and moon phase are also part of each day in the
  [daily forecast](/capabilities/weather/forecasts), which does have a method.
</Note>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Which locations are covered?">
    Sunrise, sunset, solar elevation and solar radiation work for any
    coordinate on Earth; moon data is served for cities; tides are served for
    tide stations worldwide.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How are times and timezones handled?">
    The solar-elevation tool takes an explicit timezone, and the radiation
    forecast can be switched to local time. For the other tools, check the
    time fields that `describe_tool` and the response show before converting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How fresh is the data?">
    Astronomical times are computed facts and do not change; tide tables and
    the radiation forecast are fetched from QWeather at request time.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
