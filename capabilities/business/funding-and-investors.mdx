Who funded a company, at what stage, alongside whom, and what happened next —
acquisition, IPO or quiet closure — is the core of venture research, and it
lives in Crunchbase's funding, investment and transaction records. Through
AgentKey your agent reads those records the same way it reads everything
else — `find_tools`, `describe_tool`, `execute_tool` — from a single round to
a filtered search across every deal.

## What you can ask

- *"Who led Anthropic's latest round, and how much did it raise?"*
- *"List the Series A rounds in climate tech announced this quarter."*
- *"Which investors most often co-invest with a16z?"*
- *"Has this company been acquired, and by whom?"*
- *"Which companies went public this year in Europe?"*
- *"Is this startup likely to raise again soon?"*
- *"Which funds has Sequoia raised, and who invested in them?"*
- *"这家公司最近一轮融资的投资方有哪些？"*

## What's covered

**Funding rounds**
- Funding round lookup, with cards for investments, investors, lead investors,
  the funded organization, partners and press references
- Search funding rounds with complex filters expressed in the request body

**Investments and investors**
- Individual investment lookup, with cards for the funding round, investor,
  organization and partner
- Search investments
- Investor insights and investor matches — lookup and search

**Funds**
- Fund lookup, with cards for the fund's investors, its owner and press
  references
- Search funds

**Acquisitions and IPOs**
- Acquisition lookup, with cards for the acquiree, the acquirer and press
  references; search acquisitions
- IPO lookup, with cards for the organization and press references; search
  IPOs

**Predictions and insights**
- Funding, acquisition, IPO, growth, closure, layoff and remain-private
  predictions — lookup and search
- Growth insights and research insights — lookup and search

## How to use it

Pass the request in plain language and let the router pick the tool:

```text
find_tools(q="funding rounds raised by Anthropic with lead investors")
find_tools(q="今年欧洲上市的公司")
find_tools(prefix="business/crunchbase")  # browse every Crunchbase entity type
describe_tool("<returned Provider/Operation>")
execute_tool("<Provider/Operation>", { ...params })
```

Both English and Chinese phrasing work. Always `describe_tool` before
executing — search tools take a structured query body, card lookups take a
card name, and the per-call cost is shown there. Resolve the organization or
person identifier first.

<Note>
  **Building with code?** The same capability is available as a fixed-contract
  [Scenario Method](/api-reference/scenario/overview) with automatic provider
  failover:
  [`business.company.funding`](/api-reference/scenario/methods/business-company-funding)
  lists the funding rounds a company raised, newest first.
</Note>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does this cover crypto fundraising?">
    Crunchbase records crypto companies as it does any other. For token
    projects, VC funds and rounds specific to Web3, see
    [Crypto projects and VCs](/capabilities/business/crypto-projects-and-vcs),
    backed by RootData.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What are the prediction entities?">
    Crunchbase-derived forward-looking signals — whether an organization is
    likely to raise, be acquired, go public, grow, close, lay off staff or
    remain private — exposed as entities you can look up for one company or
    search across many.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is historical data available?">
    Yes. Funding rounds, investments, acquisitions and IPOs are dated
    historical records, and the search tools filter on those dates.
    Everything is fetched at request time.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
